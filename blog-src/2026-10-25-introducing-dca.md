Institutions are moving into digital assets faster than they are training the people who will look after them. A bank that adds custody, a fund that holds tokens for clients, an exchange preparing for a licence: each one needs staff who understand how keys are generated, stored, used and recovered, and how an auditor will check every one of those steps.

That knowledge is scarce, and most of it is learned on the job, after something has already gone wrong. Digital Custody Academy exists to teach it beforehand.

## What we mean by digital custody

When a firm holds digital assets for someone else, what it really holds is the ability to sign. Whoever controls the private key controls the asset, and there is no bank behind the bank to reverse a mistake. Custody is the discipline of making sure that ability is exercised only by the right people, under the right controls, with a record of every use, and that it survives the loss of a device, a building or an employee.

That discipline spans cryptography, infrastructure, operations and regulation. A board member needs to understand the risks well enough to ask the right questions. A security engineer needs to design the key ceremony. A compliance officer needs to show an examiner that the controls exist and work. They need different depth, but they need to share a vocabulary.

## Aligned to the standards auditors use

Every module maps its lessons to the controls of a recognised standard, so what you learn is what you will be measured against:

- **CCSS v9.0**, the CryptoCurrency Security Standard, for key generation, storage, use and audit.
- **CMTA DACS**, the Digital Assets Custody Standard from the Capital Markets and Technology Association, for custody operations and governance.
- **NIST CSF 2.0**, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, which gives the curriculum its structure for risk, controls and response.

## Four modules

The curriculum is organised into four modules, each written for a different part of the organisation:

1. **Foundations of Digital Custody** covers blockchain architecture, key management fundamentals, hot and cold storage, and custody operating frameworks. It is written for board members, institutional sales leads, compliance officers and operations managers.
2. **Enterprise Key Management** goes deep on key generation, rotation and recovery, HSM integration, multisig and threshold schemes. It is for CTOs, security engineers and infrastructure architects.
3. **Security Architecture for Custody** addresses segmentation, access control, monitoring, incident response, disaster recovery and audit trails, for the engineers who build the platform.
4. **Compliance and Operational Security** covers regulatory frameworks, internal controls and the procedures a custody team needs before an examination.

All four are in preparation. Each will be published when it is ready.

## Online, and on your own machine

Most lessons will be available online, in your browser. Some need more than a browser can safely offer: working with a hardware wallet, a smart card or another native interface. Those lessons will run inside [Sanctum](https://sanctum.enigma.sh), a desktop application that runs signed tools in a sandbox. You install Sanctum, add the DCA registry and install the lesson, and it runs on your own computer.

This split is deliberate. A lesson that touches keys and devices should not ask you to trust a server you cannot inspect, and a lesson installed as a signed file stays the same file until you choose to update it.

## A record you sign

Completion is a document, not a checkbox. When you finish a module, you receive a completion record and sign it through an identity provider. That signature ties the certificate to the person who completed the course, so anyone you show it to can check that it is yours. The first supported courses will be in the UAE, signed with UAE PASS.
